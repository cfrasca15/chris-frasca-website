Self-employed? Know your enrollment dates

If you work for yourself or run a small business, your health coverage may not work the way an employer plan does. According to CMS, coverage through the Individual Health Insurance Marketplace does not give you a Special Enrollment Period for Medicare.

That makes your Initial Enrollment Period, the 7 months around your 65th birthday, especially important. I walk you through your dates so you can avoid a gap in coverage or a late-enrollment penalty.

Medicare questions from Laguna Beach residents

I buy my own health insurance. When do I need to sign up for Medicare?

Generally during your Initial Enrollment Period, which starts 3 months before the month you turn 65 and ends 3 months after. Medicare.gov explains that group coverage through current work may allow you to wait, but individual or Marketplace coverage does not work the same way, so check your situation early.

What happens if I wait too long?

In most cases, if you do not sign up for Part B when you are first eligible, you pay a late enrollment penalty for as long as you have Part B. The turning-65 guide on this site explains it with sources.

Can you help by phone or video?

Yes. Phone, video, and in-person meetings are all available, and family members are welcome to join.

What does it cost to work with you?

Nothing. Licensed insurance agents are paid a commission by the insurance carrier, not by you. Your premium is the same whether you enroll through an agent or directly with Medicare, and there is no fee for a consultation, plan review, or yearly review.
