Switching from Medicare Advantage to Medigap in California

When you can leave a Medicare Advantage plan, when a Medigap insurer can ask about your health, and the situations where it can’t.

Last updated November 12, 2026. Facts come from Medicare.gov and KFF and are linked in the sources below.

The short version: You can leave a Medicare Advantage plan and go back to Original Medicare at set times each year. Getting a Medigap policy is a separate step. Outside your one-time Medigap enrollment window, a Medigap insurer can usually ask about your health, and it can charge you more or turn you down, unless you have a guaranteed issue right. So check the Medigap side first, before you leave your plan.

When you can leave Medicare Advantage

  • Annual Enrollment, October 15 to December 7. Changes start January 1.
  • January 1 to March 31, if you are already in a Medicare Advantage plan. You can switch to another Medicare Advantage plan, or drop it and go back to Original Medicare, and you can also join a separate Medicare drug plan. Coverage starts the first of the month after the plan gets your request.
  • Special Enrollment Periods. Certain events, like moving out of your plan’s area or your plan ending, can give you another chance to change.

Source: Medicare.gov, Joining a plan

Why Medigap is the harder part

Your one-time Medigap enrollment window lasts 6 months and starts the first month you are 65 or older and signed up for Part B. Outside that window, Medicare.gov says there is no federal guarantee that an insurance company will sell you a Medigap policy, and it may cost more because of past or present health problems.

California’s birthday rule doesn’t help here. It lets people who already have a Medigap policy switch each year to one with the same or fewer benefits without health questions. It does not let someone without Medigap buy a new policy.

Sources: Medicare.gov, When can I buy a Medigap policy?; KFF, Medigap Guaranteed Issue Requirements: An Overview of Federal and State Rules

When a Medigap insurer can’t turn you down

A guaranteed issue right is a protection that comes from a specific event, like your Medicare Advantage plan ending. If you have one and apply in time, the Medigap insurer:

  • Has to sell you the policy. It can’t turn you down because of your health.
  • Can’t charge you more because of your health. Medicare.gov says the company can’t use answers to medical questions to deny you a policy or change the price.
  • Can’t make you wait for coverage of a health condition you already have.

A few things to know:

  • Each right has a deadline. If you miss it, the insurer can go back to asking health questions.
  • You need proof. Medicare.gov says you give the company proof of your situation, like copies of letters, notices, or emails. Keep everything your plan sends you.
  • It covers certain Medigap plans, not every plan. Which ones depends on the right and on when you became eligible for Medicare.
  • Prices still differ between companies. A guaranteed issue right means you can’t be turned down or charged more for your health. Each company still sets its own premiums, so it is worth reviewing more than one.
  • If you have Medicaid (Medi-Cal), Medicare.gov says insurance companies generally can’t sell you a Medigap policy.

The main federal guaranteed issue rights for people leaving Medicare Advantage:

  • Your plan is ending or leaving your area, or you move out of its area. Medicare.gov says you can apply as early as 60 days before your Medicare Advantage coverage ends, and no later than 63 days after it ends.
  • You joined Medicare Advantage when you first became eligible at 65, and you leave within the first year. You can buy a Medigap policy sold in California.
  • You dropped a Medigap policy to join Medicare Advantage, and you leave within the first year. You can generally buy back the same Medigap policy, if that insurer still sells it.

Which Medigap plans you can buy with a guaranteed issue right depends on the right and on when you became eligible for Medicare. Plans C and F are only for people who were first eligible for Medicare before January 1, 2020.

Sources: Medicare.gov, How to buy a Medigap policy, When can I buy a Medigap policy? and Compare Medigap plan benefits; KFF, Medigap Guaranteed Issue Requirements: An Overview of Federal and State Rules

California adds many more

California law gives people more guaranteed issue situations than federal law does. Medigap insurers in California list more than a dozen of them on their applications, and each one has its own rules about timing and which plans you can buy.

If your plan is changing or ending, or your doctor or medical group is leaving your plan’s network, ask me which guaranteed issue rights may apply to you. I check this before you change anything.

How to switch without a gap

  1. Check the Medigap side first. Find out whether you have a guaranteed issue right, or whether you would need to answer health questions.
  2. Apply for Medigap before you leave your plan. Wait until the Medigap policy is approved before you give up your Medicare Advantage plan.
  3. Add a drug plan. Original Medicare doesn’t cover most prescriptions, so you join a separate Medicare drug plan (Part D).
  4. Line up the start dates. Your Medigap policy and drug plan should start the same day your Medicare Advantage plan ends, usually January 1.
  5. Keep every letter from your plan. If your plan is ending, the letter can help show you have a guaranteed issue right.

Common questions

Can I switch from Medicare Advantage to Medigap any time?

You can leave Medicare Advantage during Annual Enrollment (October 15 to December 7), from January 1 to March 31 if you are already in a Medicare Advantage plan, or during a Special Enrollment Period. Buying Medigap is a separate step. Outside your first Medigap enrollment window, the insurer can usually ask about your health unless you have a guaranteed issue right.

Will I have to answer health questions?

Usually, unless you have a guaranteed issue right. Examples include your Medicare Advantage plan ending or leaving your area, or leaving Medicare Advantage within your first year after joining at 65. I check which applies to you before you change anything.

Does California's birthday rule let me switch from Medicare Advantage to Medigap?

No. The birthday rule is for people who already have a Medigap policy. Each year it gives them 60 days, starting on their birthday, to switch to a Medigap policy with the same or fewer benefits without answering health questions.

My plan is ending for 2027. Can I get Medigap?

Possibly. Because your plan is ending, you may qualify for guaranteed issue into a Medigap plan, which means no health questions. Whether it applies to you depends on your situation, so I check it with you before you change anything.
