The short answer

A hospital taking Medicare is not the same as your doctor being in your plan

  • Original Medicare with a Medigap plan: you can see any provider that accepts Medicare. There is no network and no referral requirement.
  • Medicare Advantage: the plan has a network. In California that network often depends on the medical group your doctor belongs to, as well as the hospital.
  • Plan lists change every year. A plan that works with a hospital or medical group this year may not next year, so it is worth checking each fall during Annual Enrollment, October 15 to December 7.

Kaiser Permanente

  • Orange County medical centers in Anaheim and Irvine.
  • Kaiser is both the health plan and the care provider, so it works differently from the others. Kaiser’s own 2027 plan documents explain how its Senior Advantage plan works and what you would pay.
The medical group layer

Why your doctor’s medical group matters

In California, many Medicare Advantage HMO plans contract with medical groups, and your primary care doctor belongs to one. A hospital can accept a plan while your doctor’s group does not, or the other way around. That is why a plan can look fine on the hospital’s page and still not include your doctor.

When I review plans with you, I check your doctor by name, the medical group, and the hospital you would use, against each plan.

If your plan changes

What to do if your plan will no longer include your doctor or hospital

  1. Read your Annual Notice of Change or the notice your plan sent. It says what is changing.
  2. Make a list of your doctors, your hospital, your prescriptions and doses, and your pharmacy.
  3. Check each item against the plans you are considering. Ask for the doctor’s name and the medical group, not only the hospital.
  4. Decide before December 7 if you want the change to start January 1. If you miss it, you may qualify for a special enrollment period, depending on your situation.
  5. Do not cancel your current plan until the new coverage and its start date are confirmed.

If your plan is ending altogether, see my pages for Aetna PPO members and Providence Sycamore members.

Original Medicare and Medigap

Choosing a system without a network

Original Medicare lets you see any provider that accepts Medicare, at any of these systems, with no network and no referrals. A Medigap (Supplement) plan helps with out-of-pocket costs, and a separate Part D plan covers prescriptions. Medigap may ask health questions, though some people qualify without them. I will check which applies to you.

Medigap premiums are usually higher than a Medicare Advantage plan, so it is worth looking at the total cost as well as your doctors.

Common questions

Questions about Orange County health systems and Medicare

Does Hoag take my Medicare Advantage plan?

It depends on the plan and your doctor’s medical group. Hoag lists the plans it accepts on its Medicare page and says they can change every year. I check your plan, your doctor and your hospital together before you enroll.

If a hospital accepts my plan, is my doctor covered too?

Not always. In California a doctor belongs to a medical group, and a plan can include the hospital without including that group. That is why I check your doctor by name as well as the hospital.

Can I keep my doctor if my plan is ending?

It depends on the plan you move to. Some Medicare Advantage plans may include your doctor and some may not, and Original Medicare with a Medigap plan lets you see any provider that accepts Medicare. I check each of your doctors against the options before you decide.

Do I need a referral to see a specialist?

Many Medicare Advantage HMO plans ask you to use a medical group and may require a referral for a specialist. Original Medicare does not require referrals. I explain how each option works for you.

Which health system is best for Medicare?

I do not rank them. The right fit depends on where you live, which doctors you see, your prescriptions and what you can afford. I show you the options and the costs, and you decide.

How do I check whether my doctor is in a plan?

Ask the plan, and ask your doctor’s office which plans it accepts for the year you are enrolling in. Give both the doctor’s name and the medical group. I do this with you for each option I review at no cost.
