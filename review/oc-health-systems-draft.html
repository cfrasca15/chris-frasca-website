The short answer

A hospital taking your plan is not the same as your doctor being in it

  • Original Medicare with a Medigap plan: you can see any provider that accepts Medicare at any of these systems. There is no network and no referral requirement.
  • Medicare Advantage: the plan has a network. In California that network usually depends on the medical group your doctor belongs to, as well as the hospital.
  • Lists change every year. A plan that works with a hospital or medical group this year may not next year, so it is worth checking each fall during Annual Enrollment, October 15 to December 7.
  • Before you enroll, confirm two things: the plan with the carrier, and your doctor with the doctor’s office or medical group. I do both with you at no cost.
The main systems

The health systems and medical groups most Orange County members ask about

This is a guide to how each system is set up, plus the 2027 network changes each one has published. It is not a list of every plan. Each section links the system’s own page, where the current plan list is kept.

Hoag

Hospitals: Hoag Hospital Newport Beach and Hoag Hospital Irvine. Medical groups: Hoag Medical Group and Hoag Physician Partners.

  • Hoag lists the Medicare Advantage plans Hoag Medical Group accepts. It says specialists in Hoag Physician Partners may participate in other plans, and that participation can change at the plan’s discretion.
  • Hoag’s contract with SCAN ends December 31, 2026, so SCAN HMO members will find Hoag out of network starting January 1, 2027. Hoag’s page also names plans that start January 1, 2027. I wrote a separate page on the SCAN change: Hoag and SCAN for 2027.
  • Hoag’s list covers the medical group. Confirm the hospital with your plan as well.

Source: Hoag, Insurance Accepted and Hoag, Medicare.

Providence

Hospitals: Providence St. Joseph Hospital Orange, Providence St. Jude Medical Center, and Providence Mission Hospital (Mission Viejo and Laguna Beach). Medical groups: St. Joseph Heritage Medical Group, St. Jude Heritage Medical Group and Mission Heritage Medical Group, plus the Providence Affiliated Physicians groups.

  • Providence says that for you to use one of its hospitals, both your physician group and your health plan must be contracted with that hospital.
  • Providence has said that effective January 1, 2026, the Providence Clinical Network, which includes its Orange County hospitals and the Heritage medical groups, is no longer contracted with UnitedHealthcare Medicare Advantage plans.
  • Providence Health Plan, the insurance company, will not offer Medicare Advantage plans in 2027. That is the insurance plan, not the hospitals or doctors, which may still be available under other plans. If you are a Providence Sycamore member, see the Providence Sycamore page.

Source: Providence Southern California, Insurance Accepted, Providence, UnitedHealthcare Medicare Advantage contract termination and Providence Health Plan, status update.

UCI Health

Locations: UCI Medical Center in Orange and UCI Health Irvine, among others. Medical group: UCI University Physicians & Surgeons.

  • UCI Health says it accepts Medicare, Medicare with a Medicare Supplement, and several Medicare Advantage plans. Its Medicare page lists the Medicare Advantage plans it will accept beginning January 1, 2027, by PPO and HMO.
  • UCI Health says the list may change and that you should call your insurance carrier to confirm.

Source: UCI Health, Medicare insurance information.

MemorialCare

Orange County hospitals: Orange Coast Medical Center in Fountain Valley and Saddleback Medical Center in Laguna Hills. Medical groups: MemorialCare Medical Group, MemorialCare Independent Physicians and Edinger Medical Group.

  • MemorialCare lists its Medicare Advantage plans separately for each medical group, so a plan accepted by one group may not be accepted by another.
  • Its page flags some Medicare Advantage HMO plans, including Aetna and Blue Shield, as no longer accepted in 2027, depending on the medical group.
  • MemorialCare’s hospital pages use older plan names, so for a current answer go by your medical group’s list and confirm the hospital with your plan.

Source: MemorialCare, Health Insurance Plans.

Plans and contracts change. Before you enroll, confirm with the plan and with your doctor’s medical group. I do that with you.

The medical group layer

Why your doctor’s medical group matters

In California, many Medicare Advantage HMO plans contract with medical groups, and your primary care doctor belongs to one. A hospital can accept a plan while your doctor’s group does not, or the other way around. MemorialCare’s page shows this clearly: the same carrier can be accepted by one of its medical groups and marked as ending for another.

When I review plans with you, I check your doctor by name, the medical group, and the hospital you would use, against each plan.

If your plan changes

What to do if your plan will no longer include your doctor or hospital

  1. Read your Annual Notice of Change or the notice your plan sent. It says what is changing.
  2. Make a list of your doctors, your hospital, your prescriptions and doses, and your pharmacy.
  3. Check each item against the plans you are considering. Ask for the doctor’s name and the medical group, not only the hospital.
  4. Decide before December 7 if you want the change to start January 1. If you miss it, you may qualify for a special enrollment period, depending on your situation.
  5. Do not cancel your current plan until the new coverage and its start date are confirmed.

If your plan is ending altogether, see my pages for Aetna PPO members and Providence Sycamore members.

Original Medicare and Medigap

Choosing a system without a network

Original Medicare lets you see any provider that accepts Medicare, at any of these systems, with no network and no referrals. A Medigap (Supplement) plan helps with out-of-pocket costs, and a separate Part D plan covers prescriptions. Medigap may ask health questions, though you may qualify without them in some situations. I will check which applies to you.

Medigap premiums are usually higher than a Medicare Advantage plan, so it is worth looking at the total cost as well as your doctors.

Common questions

Questions about Orange County health systems and Medicare

What is a medical group and why does it matter for Medicare Advantage?

A medical group is the organization your primary care doctor belongs to. In California, many Medicare Advantage HMO plans contract with medical groups, so a plan can include a hospital without including your doctor’s group. That is why I check your doctor by name and medical group, not only the hospital.

Does Hoag take my Medicare Advantage plan?

It depends on the plan and your doctor’s medical group. Hoag lists the plans Hoag Medical Group accepts on its own site and says they can change. Hoag’s contract with SCAN ends December 31, 2026, so SCAN HMO members will find Hoag out of network starting January 1, 2027. I confirm your plan, your doctor and your hospital together before you enroll.

Are Providence hospitals and doctors still available if I change plans?

Providence Health Plan, the insurance company, will not offer Medicare Advantage plans in 2027. That is separate from the Providence hospitals and doctors, which may still be available under other plans. Providence has also said its Orange County hospitals and Heritage medical groups stopped contracting with UnitedHealthcare Medicare Advantage plans on January 1, 2026. I check each of your doctors before you enroll.

If a hospital accepts my plan, is my doctor covered too?

Not always. In California a doctor belongs to a medical group, and a plan can include the hospital without including that group. MemorialCare’s page shows the same carrier accepted by one of its medical groups and marked as ending for another.

Can I keep my doctor if my plan is ending?

It depends on the plan you move to. Some Medicare Advantage plans may include your doctor and some may not, and Original Medicare with a Medigap plan lets you see any provider that accepts Medicare. I check each of your doctors against the options before you decide.

Do I need a referral to see a specialist?

Many Medicare Advantage HMO plans ask you to use a medical group and may require a referral for a specialist. Original Medicare does not require referrals. I explain how each option works for you.

Which health system is right for Medicare?

I do not rank them. The right fit depends on where you live, which doctors you see, your prescriptions and what you can afford. I show you the options and the costs, and you decide.

How do I check whether my doctor is in a plan?

Ask the plan, and ask your doctor’s office which plans it accepts for the year you are enrolling in. Give both the doctor’s name and the medical group. I do this with you for each option I review at no cost.
