Enrollment periods

Medicare Advantage Open Enrollment Period: January 1 to March 31

What this period is, who can use it, and what you can and can't change, in plain English and with every fact linked to its source.

The short version: The Medicare Advantage Open Enrollment Period runs from January 1 to March 31 each year. It is only for people who are already in a Medicare Advantage plan. During it you can make one change: switch to a different Medicare Advantage plan, or go back to Original Medicare and join a separate Medicare drug plan.

Who can use it

This period is only for people who are already in a Medicare Advantage plan. If you have Original Medicare, with or without a Medigap policy or a separate drug plan, this period doesn't apply to you.

If you want to move from Original Medicare to a Medicare Advantage plan, that happens during Annual Enrollment, October 15 to December 7, or with a Special Enrollment Period if you qualify for one.

Source: Medicare.gov, Joining a plan

What you can change

  • Switch to another Medicare Advantage plan, with or without drug coverage.
  • Drop your Medicare Advantage plan and return to Original Medicare. You can also join a separate Medicare drug plan (Part D).

You get one change. The federal rule lets a person in a Medicare Advantage plan make one election during the first 3 months of the year. Changes made during Annual Enrollment or a Special Enrollment Period don't count against it.

When it starts: your new coverage begins the first of the month after the plan gets your request. A request the plan receives in February starts March 1.

Sources: Medicare.gov, Joining a plan; Code of Federal Regulations, 42 CFR 422.62(a)(3). The example applies those rules.

Three enrollment periods that get mixed up

Two different periods run from January 1 to March 31, and neither is the fall Annual Enrollment. Here is how they differ.

PeriodDatesWho it is forWhat you can do
Annual Enrollment (also called Medicare Open Enrollment)October 15 to December 7People with MedicareJoin, drop, or switch Medicare Advantage and Medicare drug plans. Changes start January 1.
Medicare Advantage Open Enrollment PeriodJanuary 1 to March 31People already in a Medicare Advantage planOne change: another Medicare Advantage plan, or back to Original Medicare with a separate drug plan.
General Enrollment PeriodJanuary 1 to March 31People who missed their first chance to sign up for Part A or Part B and don't qualify for a Special Enrollment PeriodSign up for Part A or Part B. Coverage starts the month after you sign up.

Sources: Medicare.gov, Joining a plan and When does Medicare coverage start?

New to Medicare? You have a similar window

If you joined a Medicare Advantage plan during your Initial Enrollment Period, you can change to another Medicare Advantage plan or go back to Original Medicare within the first 3 months you have Medicare Part A and Part B. For more on your first enrollment window, see my turning 65 guide.

Source: Medicare.gov, Joining a plan

Going back to Original Medicare? Look at Medigap first

Leaving a Medicare Advantage plan is one step. Buying a Medicare Supplement (Medigap) policy to go with Original Medicare is a separate one, and it has its own rules.

Your one-time Medigap enrollment window lasts 6 months and starts the first month you are 65 or older and signed up for Part B. Outside that window, there is no federal guarantee that an insurance company will sell you a Medigap policy, and it may cost more because of past or present health problems, unless you have a guaranteed issue right. California law adds more guaranteed issue situations than federal law does.

So if Medigap is part of your plan, find out where you stand with it before you leave your Medicare Advantage plan. My guide to switching from Medicare Advantage to Medigap in California walks through it.

Source: Medicare.gov, When can I buy a Medigap policy?

What to check before you change anything

Because you only get one change in this period, it helps to check a few things first.

  • Your doctors. Check each one by name, and check their medical group too. In California, many Medicare Advantage HMOs work through medical groups.
  • Your medications and pharmacy. Look at whether each prescription is covered and what it costs at the pharmacy you use.
  • Your costs. Look at the premium, the copays for the care you use most, and the yearly out-of-pocket maximum.
  • What you have already paid this year. Amounts you have paid toward a deductible or out-of-pocket maximum may not carry over to a new plan. I can help you check.
  • Medigap, if you are thinking about Original Medicare. Check it first, as described above.

Other times you can change coverage

  • Special Enrollment Periods. These are only for certain situations that happen in your life, like moving to a new address.
  • Annual Enrollment, October 15 to December 7. Changes start January 1.

Source: Medicare.gov, Joining a plan

Common questions

When is the Medicare Advantage Open Enrollment Period?

It runs from January 1 to March 31 each year. It is only for people who are already in a Medicare Advantage plan.

Is it the same as Annual Enrollment?

No. Annual Enrollment runs October 15 to December 7 and is open to people with Medicare in general. The Medicare Advantage Open Enrollment Period runs January 1 to March 31, is only for people already in a Medicare Advantage plan, and allows one change.

Can I use it if I have Original Medicare?

No. This period is only for people who are already in a Medicare Advantage plan. Switching from Original Medicare to a Medicare Advantage plan is something you can do during Annual Enrollment, October 15 to December 7, or with a Special Enrollment Period if you qualify for one.

How many changes can I make?

One. The federal rule lets a person in a Medicare Advantage plan make one election during the first 3 months of the year.

When does my new coverage start?

The first of the month after the plan gets your request. A request the plan receives in February starts March 1.

Can I go back to Original Medicare and buy a Medigap policy?

You can go back to Original Medicare during this period. Buying a Medigap policy is a separate step. Outside your one-time Medigap enrollment window, the insurer can usually ask about your health unless you have a guaranteed issue right, so check the Medigap side before you leave your plan.
