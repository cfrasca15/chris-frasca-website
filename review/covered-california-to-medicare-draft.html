Turning 65 on Covered California: Moving to Medicare

When your financial help ends, when to sign up for Medicare, and how to cancel your Covered California plan without a gap or a double bill.

Last updated November 20, 2026. Facts come from Covered California, HealthCare.gov and Medicare.gov and are linked in the sources below.

The short version: Covered California doesn't move you to Medicare, and it doesn't cancel your plan for you. Sign up for Medicare during your Initial Enrollment Period, tell Covered California within 30 days, and cancel your Covered California plan at least 14 days before you want it to end. Once you are eligible for Medicare, Covered California says you can't get financial help with its plans.

What changes when you become eligible for Medicare

  • Your financial help ends. Covered California says people who are eligible for Medicare aren't eligible for financial help to lower the monthly premium, even if they don't enroll in Medicare. HealthCare.gov says you would have to pay full price for a Marketplace plan.
  • Your plan is not canceled for you. Covered California says your plan won't be automatically canceled when you become eligible for Medicare, even if you enroll in a Medicare plan with the same health insurance company.
  • Covered California does not sell Medicare plans. Signing up for Medicare happens through Social Security, and choosing how you get your Medicare coverage is a separate step.

Sources: Covered California, Moving to Medicare; HealthCare.gov, Changing from the Marketplace to Medicare

Step by step

  1. Find your dates. Your Initial Enrollment Period is a 7-month window that starts 3 months before the month you turn 65. If you sign up before your birthday month, your Medicare coverage starts the month you turn 65. My turning 65 guide has the full timeline.
  2. Sign up for Part A and Part B. If you already get Social Security benefits you are enrolled automatically. If not, you sign up with Social Security.
  3. Decide how you want to get your Medicare coverage. For example, Original Medicare with a Medigap policy and a Part D drug plan, or a Medicare Advantage plan. See the differences.
  4. Tell Covered California. It asks you to report your Medicare eligibility within 30 days.
  5. Cancel your Covered California plan. You must cancel at least 14 days before you want it to end. You can do it in your online account or by calling Covered California at (800) 300-1506.
  6. Line up the dates. In the HealthCare.gov example, Medicare starts May 1 and the Marketplace coverage ends April 30, the day before.
  7. Check on your family's coverage. If a spouse or other family members are staying on Covered California, confirm their plan after you make the change.

Sources: Medicare.gov, When can I sign up for Medicare? and When does Medicare coverage start?; Covered California, Moving to Medicare; HealthCare.gov, Changing from the Marketplace to Medicare

If you keep your Covered California plan instead

Some people think about staying on the plan they know. Here is what the official sources say can happen.

  • You may have to pay back your financial help. Covered California says this applies if you are eligible for Medicare and keep your Covered California plan.
  • Your Medicare start date could be delayed. HealthCare.gov says that if you want to sign up after you are first eligible, you may have to wait until the General Enrollment Period, January 1 to March 31 each year.
  • You may pay late enrollment penalties. HealthCare.gov says that in most cases you would also pay monthly late enrollment penalties for Part B and Part D, and that they go up the longer you wait.
  • A Covered California plan is not job coverage. People who work past 65 with health insurance from a job can often wait on Part B and sign up later without a penalty. Medicare.gov treats Marketplace plans as insurance that is not from a job, and according to CMS, coverage through the Individual Health Insurance Marketplace does not give you a Special Enrollment Period. If you have coverage through an employer instead, see working past 65.

Sources: Covered California, Moving to Medicare; HealthCare.gov, Medicare and the Marketplace and Changing from the Marketplace to Medicare; Medicare.gov, Working past 65; CMS, Enrolling in Medicare Part A & Part B

Your spouse and family stay on Covered California

Medicare covers you, not your household. HealthCare.gov says others on your application keep their Marketplace coverage, and it reminds you to confirm the plan for anyone in your household who needs to keep it.

When you report the change, ask Covered California how your household's financial help changes once you are no longer on the plan.

Source: HealthCare.gov, Changing from the Marketplace to Medicare

Two situations worth a closer look

  • You would have to pay a premium for Part A. Most people get Part A with no premium because they or a spouse paid Medicare taxes while working. HealthCare.gov describes different choices for people who pay a Part A premium, including having a Marketplace plan instead. Check with Covered California and Social Security before you decide.
  • You have Medi-Cal. Medi-Cal rules can change when you turn 65 or become eligible for Medicare. Contact your county Medi-Cal office, or call HICAP, California's no-cost Medicare counseling program, at 1-800-434-0222.

Sources: HealthCare.gov, Medicare and the Marketplace; California Department of Aging, HICAP Medicare counseling

One more window to know about: Medigap

If you want a Medicare Supplement (Medigap) policy, Medicare.gov says your one-time Medigap enrollment window lasts 6 months and starts the first month you are 65 or older and signed up for Part B. After that period, your options to buy a Medigap policy may be limited and the policy may cost more.

Source: Medicare.gov, When can I buy a Medigap policy?

Common questions

Does Covered California switch me to Medicare when I turn 65?

No. Covered California does not sell Medicare plans, and it says your plan won't be automatically canceled when you become eligible for Medicare. You sign up for Medicare through Social Security and cancel your Covered California plan yourself.

When should I cancel my Covered California plan?

Covered California says you must cancel at least 14 days before you want the plan to end. The goal is for it to end the day before your Medicare coverage starts, so there is no gap and no overlap.

Can I keep my Covered California plan instead of Medicare?

Covered California says people who are eligible for Medicare aren't eligible for financial help with a Covered California plan, even if they don't enroll in Medicare. If you keep the plan, you may have to pay back financial help, and you may pay late enrollment penalties when you do sign up for Medicare.

What happens to my spouse's Covered California coverage?

HealthCare.gov says others on your application keep their Marketplace coverage. Report the change to Covered California, and ask how the household's financial help changes once you move to Medicare.

I have Medi-Cal, not a Covered California plan. What changes at 65?

Medi-Cal rules can change when you turn 65 or become eligible for Medicare. Contact your county Medi-Cal office, or call HICAP, California's no-cost Medicare counseling program, at 1-800-434-0222.
