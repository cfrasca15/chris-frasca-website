Part D in 2027

  • Maximum deductible: $700 in 2027, up from $615 in 2026.
  • Out-of-pocket threshold: $2,400 in 2027, up from $2,100 in 2026.
  • CMS's rules for 2027 also remove cost sharing for enrollees in the catastrophic phase.
  • Medicare Prescription Payment Plan: this option spreads your drug costs across the year. Medicare.gov notes that it doesn't save you money or lower your drug costs.

Sources: CMS 2027 Rate Announcement (Table V-2), Medicare.gov, and the CMS 2027 final rule fact sheet.

Medicare Advantage in 2027

Plans can change their premiums, benefits, provider networks, and costs each year. The averages above hide a lot of variation from plan to plan, so the notice your plan sends is the best guide to what changes for you. Look for any change in your doctors, your hospital, your drugs, and your out-of-pocket costs.

What stays the same

  • The Annual Enrollment dates, October 15 through December 7.
  • You don't need to re-enroll to keep your current Medicare coverage, according to CMS.
  • 1-800-MEDICARE is available 24 hours a day, seven days a week.

What this page doesn't cover

This page doesn't list 2027 Medicare Part B premiums or deductibles. Check Medicare.gov for those figures when they are published.

Your Annual Enrollment checklist

  • Read the Annual Notice of Change from your plan. It should be in your mail.
  • Write down your doctors, hospital, medications, and pharmacy.
  • Check whether your plan's 2027 costs and network still fit.
  • Review other options, using the Medicare Plan Finder on Medicare.gov or by talking with a licensed agent.
  • Make any change before December 7. If your income is limited, ask about Medicare Savings Programs and Extra Help.

Common questions

When is Medicare Annual Enrollment in 2026?

The Annual Enrollment Period runs from October 15, 2026 through December 7, 2026. Changes you make take effect on January 1, 2027.

Do I need to do anything if I like my plan?

According to CMS, people who want to keep their current Medicare coverage do not need to re-enroll. Plans can still change their costs, benefits, and networks each year, so it is worth reading the Annual Notice of Change your plan sends you.

What is the Part D out-of-pocket limit for 2027?

According to CMS, the Part D out-of-pocket threshold is $2,400 in 2027, up from $2,100 in 2026, and the maximum Part D deductible is $700, up from $615.

Does it cost anything to work with an agent?

No. Licensed insurance agents are paid a commission by the insurance carrier, not by you. Your premium is the same whether you enroll through an agent or directly with Medicare.
