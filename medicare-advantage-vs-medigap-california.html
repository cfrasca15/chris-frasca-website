Medicare Advantage vs. Medigap

Two different ways to build your coverage, side by side in plain English, with every fact linked to Medicare.gov.

The short version: Once you have Part A and Part B, there are two main ways to get the rest of your coverage. You can stay with Original Medicare and add a Medigap policy and a separate drug plan, or you can choose a Medicare Advantage plan that bundles your coverage. You can't have Medigap with a Medicare Advantage plan. Neither is better for everyone, so the right one depends on your doctors, your health, and your budget.

The two paths

  • Original Medicare, plus Medigap and a drug plan (optional). You keep Original Medicare, and you can add a Medigap (Medicare Supplement) policy from a private company to help pay costs Medicare doesn't, such as your 20% coinsurance. You join a separate Medicare drug plan for prescriptions.
  • Medicare Advantage. A private plan that replaces how you get your Medicare coverage. You still pay your Part B premium, and most plans include drug coverage.

Questions to ask yourself

  • Are your doctors and hospital in the plan's network? If you want to keep seeing any provider that takes Medicare, that points one way. If your doctors are in a plan's network, that changes the picture.
  • How do you like to pay for care? Some people prefer a higher monthly premium for fewer surprises. Others prefer a lower monthly premium and pay more when they use care. Look at both the monthly cost and what you would pay if you needed a lot of care.
  • Do the extras matter to you? Some Medicare Advantage plans offer vision, hearing, and dental services that Original Medicare doesn't cover.
  • How much do you travel? Medicare.gov says neither path generally covers medical care outside the U.S.
  • What drugs do you take? Check that your medications and pharmacy are covered under any drug plan, whether it is separate or part of a Medicare Advantage plan.

Timing matters for Medigap

Your one-time Medigap Open Enrollment Period lasts 6 months and starts the first month you are 65 or older and signed up for Part B. After that period, Medicare.gov says your options to buy a Medigap policy may be limited and the policy may cost more. If you are turning 65, read the turning-65 guide.

Source: Medicare.gov, When can I buy a Medigap policy?

You can review your choice each year

People with Medicare can review their coverage and make changes during Annual Enrollment, October 15 through December 7. Changes take effect January 1. See what CMS has published for 2027 in California.

Common questions

Can I have both a Medigap policy and a Medicare Advantage plan?

No. According to Medicare.gov, if you have Medicare Advantage you cannot buy Medigap to cover your out-of-pocket costs. Medigap works with Original Medicare.

Which one lets me see any doctor?

Medicare.gov says that with Original Medicare you can use any doctor or hospital that takes Medicare, anywhere in the U.S. With Medicare Advantage you may need to use providers in the plan's network. Medigap is added to Original Medicare, so it does not add a network.

Do I need a separate drug plan?

With Original Medicare you join a separate Medicare drug plan (Part D). According to Medicare.gov, most Medicare Advantage plans include drug coverage, so you don't need to join a separate one.

Is there a limit on what I pay in a year?

Medicare Advantage plans have a yearly limit on what you pay for covered Medicare services. Original Medicare has no yearly limit on what you pay out of pocket, unless you have supplemental coverage such as Medigap or other insurance.

Can I change my mind later?

You can review your coverage and make changes during Annual Enrollment, October 15 through December 7, with changes taking effect January 1. If you want a Medigap policy later, Medicare.gov notes that your options may be limited and the policy may cost more once your one-time Medigap Open Enrollment Period ends.
